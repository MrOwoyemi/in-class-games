# Educational In-Class Games

Classroom games for Maths, English, Science, Computing and Creative iMedia. Computing has a drop-down for each key stage: KS3 for Years 7 and 8, and KS4 for Years 9 to 11. Every game has three versions:

- **On your own:** one pupil at a computer, with hints, explanations and a chance to practise mistakes.
- **In groups:** players or teams share one computer, taking turns or using buzzer keys.
- **Whole class:** the teacher runs it on the board, with teams, a timer, and Reveal and Next.

**Play it here: https://mrowoyemi.github.io/in-class-games/**

> **Exam boards.** Computing KS3 (Years 7 and 8) follows the [national curriculum for computing at key stage 3](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-computing-programmes-of-study). Computing KS4 (Years 9 to 11) follows the [AQA GCSE Computer Science specification (8525)](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/computer-science/gcse/computer-science-8525/specification), including AQA's wording, Boolean notation and pseudo-code. The exceptions are Typing Trainer and the everyday online safety questions in Cyber Safe, which are general skills rather than part of the specification. The English games are matched to [WJEC Eduqas GCSE English Language (C700QS)](https://www.eduqas.co.uk/qualifications/english-language-gcse/), which the English department uses: spelling, punctuation, grammar and vocabulary (AO6) and the subject terminology for analysing language (AO2), plus the three rhetorical devices the specification asks pupils to use in their own writing. Eduqas doesn't publish spelling lists, so the Year 3 to 6 lists are the national curriculum's, and the others are general lists of commonly misspelt words and spelling rules. Creative iMedia follows [OCR Cambridge Nationals in Creative iMedia (J834)](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/cambridge-nationals/creative-imedia-level-1-2-j834/): Crew Call and Who's It For? cover unit R093 (topic areas 1.2, 2.1, 2.3 and 2.4), and Guess the Logo supports the teaching of unit R094, topic area 1 (1.1). The Maths and Science games aren't matched to an exam board: they follow general curriculum topics and may not match exactly what your course requires, so check them against your own specification. This site isn't produced or endorsed by AQA, Eduqas, OCR or any other exam board.

## Using it in class

1. Open the link on the classroom computer and click **Full screen**.
2. Choose **Who's playing?** at the top: On your own, In groups or Whole class.
3. Pick a subject, then a game. Each game explains how its version works before you start.

It works best on a computer or interactive whiteboard. It also works on tablets and phones.

## The games

You can add your own content to several games: rules in FizzBuzz, spelling lists (which you can save), lines from your class reader in Device Detective, passages with your own mistakes in Proofread the Page (write each one as `[mistake|correction]`, for example `[were|was]`), and logos in Guess the Logo.

## Privacy

Everything runs in the web browser. Nothing anyone types, including pupils' names in the spelling bee, is sent anywhere. Saved word lists, practice records, typing speeds and your own logos stay on the computer that made them, and a game that's under way is kept in its browser tab, so refreshing the page doesn't lose it. The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts.

## Using it without the internet

Download `index.html` from this page (click it, then the download button) and open it in any web browser. Everything works offline, except that the fonts fall back to the computer's own.
